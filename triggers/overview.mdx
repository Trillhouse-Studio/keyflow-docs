## What Are Triggers?

Triggers turn your flows from manual tools into autonomous systems. Instead of running workflows by hand every time you need them, triggers watch for specific conditions and automatically execute your flows when those conditions are met.

Think of triggers as the "when" in your automation. Your flow defines "what happens" - triggers define "when it happens."

## Why This Matters

Manual workflow execution works great for testing and one-off tasks. But the real power of Keyflow emerges when your workflows run automatically, handling routine processes while you focus on higher-value work.

Teams that embrace trigger-based automation transform how they operate. Work becomes proactive instead of reactive. Issues get handled immediately instead of waiting for someone to notice them. Routine tasks happen automatically, freeing up time for strategic work.

The goal isn't to automate everything - it's to automate the predictable, routine work so humans can focus on creative, strategic activities that actually move the business forward.

## Manual vs Automated

**Manual execution** is perfect when you:

- Need to test a new workflow
- Process data occasionally or irregularly
- Want full control over timing and inputs
- Are experimenting with different approaches

**Automated triggers** excel when you:

- Have predictable, recurring workflows
- Want to respond to events immediately
- Need 24/7 monitoring and processing
- Want to scale beyond what manual work allows

## How Triggers Work

Every trigger connects to exactly one flow, but flows can have multiple triggers. This gives you flexibility in how you activate your automation.

For example, you might have a "Process New Leads" flow that can be triggered by:

- A schedule (every 2 hours during business hours)
- A webhook (when your website form is submitted)
- An email (when leads are forwarded to a specific address)

The flow itself doesn't change - only the conditions that activate it.

## Getting Started

The easiest way to understand triggers is to start with scheduled automation. Pick a workflow you run regularly - like a weekly report or daily data sync - and set it to run automatically.

From there, you can explore event-based triggers that respond to changes in your email, files, or databases.
